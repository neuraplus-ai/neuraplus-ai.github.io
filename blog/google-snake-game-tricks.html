Most people play Google's Snake the way they played it on a phone in 1999: hold the arrow key, grab the apple, crash into your own tail around the 30-point mark. That works for a while. The game is deeper than it looks, though, and the settings menu hides a pile of modes that change the rules completely. Below you'll find the fastest way to open it, how each mode behaves, and the habits that separate a long run from a short one. There's also a small Snake you can play right on this page, so you can try the ideas while you read.

Warm up here: mini Snake, no install

How do you play Google Snake?

Type "snake game" into Google Search and press the Play button that appears above the results. Steer with the arrow keys or W, A, S and D. Eat the apples to grow, and avoid walls and your own body. Open the settings gear to change speed, map size, colors and game mode.

🚀 Quick Start: Search snake game, click Play, and pick the "Snake" speed first. Once you can reach 20 apples without panic, try the Rabbit speed or a new mode.

The mini game above is a simplified version, not Google's. It has the three speed choices and a no-border switch so you can feel the difference before you open the real thing.

🌪️ The Blender Mode: This is the one worth knowing about. You open it from the gear icon in the top right and choose which modes to combine. Peace plus anything is a good way to learn a hard mode without losing every run.

Eight habits that actually raise your score

  • Run the edges early. A snake that hugs the border leaves the whole middle open and keeps your path predictable. It's the closest thing to a free tip.
  • Zig-zag when you get long. A snake past about 40 segments can't afford open turns. Sweep the board in rows, like mowing a lawn.
  • Don't chase every apple. Sometimes the safe move is to skip a risky apple and eat the next one. Dying for one point is a bad trade.
  • Always keep an exit. Before any turn, ask yourself where you'd go next. If the answer is "nowhere," don't turn.
  • Look ahead, not at the head. Your eyes should be on the next three squares. The head takes care of itself.
  • Lower the speed to learn a mode. Turtle isn't an insult. Learning Statue or Minesweeper at full speed is just collecting crashes.
  • Mute if sound distracts you. Press M.
  • Use the restart key. Enter or Space, no mouse.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Making tight spirals toward the center. A spiral feels organized, but it traps you the moment the snake outgrows it. Use rows instead.

A short history, for the curious

Snake didn't start on Google. The version most people remember shipped on the Nokia 6110 in 1997, and the idea is older than that, going back to arcade games from the late 1970s. Wikipedia's article on the game traces that lineage, and it's a good read if you want the full story. Google's version is a modern remake with the same heart and a lot more modes.

Build your own: Snake with new rules

The widget on this page is small on purpose. Here's a prompt for a bigger one with the Portal and Wall ideas from Google's version, written from scratch so it's original.

Write a single-file HTML game called "Serpent Lab". It's a classic
snake game on a canvas grid with these original modes selectable from
a dropdown:

1. Classic: normal rules.
2. Walls: each time the snake eats, a brick appears on a random free
   tile; touching a brick ends the game.
3. Portals: two portal tiles exist; entering one exits from the other.
   Eating moves both portals to new positions.
4. Blender: lets me tick any two of the modes above and combine them.

Also include: speed selector (slow, normal, fast), score and best score
stored in memory only, restart with Enter or Space, mute with M (use a
simple generated beep, no audio files), and on-screen arrow buttons for
phones. No libraries. Comment each function.
