Every few weeks someone posts a new "best free LLM API" list, and half of it is out of date by the time it's shared. Free tiers get cut, models rotate in and out, and the numbers people quote were often true for one afternoon last spring. So this guide does something slightly different. It tells you which providers are worth your time, shows the limits as they're currently reported, and, more usefully, shows you how to check the real numbers yourself and how to survive when a free tier runs dry.

What is the best free LLM API?

There's no single winner. Groq gives you very fast responses within a modest daily budget, OpenRouter gives you the widest choice of models behind one key, and Google's Gemini free tier gives you long context and multimodal input. Choose by your bottleneck: speed, variety or context. For anything with real users, plan on a paid tier from the start.

🚀 Quick Start: Building a prototype this weekend? Start with Groq for speed or OpenRouter for choice, keep your code on the OpenAI-compatible format, and you can swap providers later by changing two lines.

Don't trust any limit table, including this one

Limits change, and they differ by account. Every provider here gives you a way to read your real numbers:

  • Groq: the Limits page in the console, plus rate-limit headers on every response.
  • OpenRouter: a GET request to its key endpoint returns your usage and whether you're on the free tier.
  • Google AI Studio: the rate limit view for your project, since limits apply per project, not per key.

Take a screenshot of each with the date. When something breaks six weeks from now, you'll know whether the limit changed or your code did. The per-model details for Groq are in the Groq API free limits guide.

A free-tier fallback in a few lines

The smartest habit with free APIs is never relying on one. Both Groq and OpenRouter speak the OpenAI format, so you can try one and fall back to the other when you hit a rate limit. Replace the model names with current ones from each provider's console.

# pip install openai
import os, time
from openai import OpenAI, RateLimitError

PROVIDERS = [
    {"name": "groq",
     "client": OpenAI(api_key=os.environ["GROQ_API_KEY"],
                      base_url="https://api.groq.com/openai/v1"),
     "model": "openai/gpt-oss-120b"},
    {"name": "openrouter",
     "client": OpenAI(api_key=os.environ["OPENROUTER_API_KEY"],
                      base_url="https://openrouter.ai/api/v1"),
     "model": "PICK_A_MODEL_ID_ENDING_IN_:free"},
]

def ask(prompt):
    last_error = None
    for p in PROVIDERS:
        for attempt in range(2):
            try:
                r = p["client"].chat.completions.create(
                    model=p["model"],
                    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": prompt}],
                )
                return p["name"], r.choices[0].message.content
            except RateLimitError as e:
                last_error = e
                time.sleep(2 ** attempt)   # short backoff, then move on
    raise RuntimeError(f"All free tiers are rate limited: {last_error}")

print(ask("Explain a 429 error in one sentence."))

Two cautions. Retrying fast can burn a small daily budget on failed calls, so keep the backoff short and move on to the next provider. And log which provider answered, because the same prompt can behave differently on different models.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Building a launch-day feature on a free tier. A free quota is fine for a prototype and risky for a product. One busy hour can use a whole day's allowance, and free capacity can disappear without notice.

Match the free tier to your project stage

Where you are in a project changes the answer more than which provider is trendiest. A weekend hack and a public beta have very different tolerance for a surprise 429.

StageWhat mattersSensible free setup
Weekend hackZero frictionOne provider, one model, no retry logic
Demo for a clientIt must not fail liveTwo providers with the fallback above, and a cached answer ready
Private betaPredictable behaviorPin one model, log every request, keep a paid key in reserve
Public launchUptime and termsMove to paid. Keep a free tier only for staging

If OpenRouter is your pick for the beta stage, read the OpenRouter free models guide first. Its $10 threshold quietly changes your daily allowance from 50 requests to 1,000, and most people only find out after they've already hit the lower number.

What free tiers can't do

  • Guarantee uptime. Free capacity is lent out, so it can vanish or slow down at peak times.
  • Keep your data private. Google's free tier terms allow inputs to be used to improve its products, and other free offerings have their own policies. Don't send customer data to any free endpoint until you've read the terms.
  • Allow every use. Some trial keys, like Cohere's, are limited to non-commercial work.
  • Stay the same. Models appear and disappear, and several write-ups I read disagreed with each other on what was free on a given date.

If your prototype turns into something people use, the Claude free plan limits guide is a useful contrast: chat products meter by usage windows, while APIs meter by requests and tokens. Knowing which one you're dealing with saves a lot of confusion.
