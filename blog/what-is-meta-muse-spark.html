For years Meta's AI story was Llama: open-weight models that developers downloaded and fine-tuned while Meta's own chatbot lagged behind the leaders. Muse Spark is the reset. It's the first model from a new lab inside Meta, it arrived in April 2026, and by October there are already several Muse models with different licenses, which is where the confusion starts. This guide explains what Muse Spark is, how it differs from Llama, what the modes mean, and which of the later releases you can actually download.

What is Meta Muse Spark?

Muse Spark is Meta's first AI model from Meta Superintelligence Labs, launched on April 8, 2026. It's a natively multimodal reasoning model that powers Meta AI in the Meta AI app and on meta.ai, with Instant, Thinking and Contemplating modes. Later versions added a developer API and open-weight releases.

Why Meta built it, and how it differs from Llama

Meta formed Meta Superintelligence Labs in 2025 and put Alexandr Wang, formerly the CEO of Scale AI, in charge. Meta had reportedly invested about $14 billion in Scale AI for a large minority stake, so the reorganization was a big bet, not a side project. Muse Spark is the first public result.

The easy mistake is treating Muse as Llama's next version. It isn't. Llama was an open-weight family, and Muse Spark launched as a proprietary model you could use through Meta's products, with developer access arriving later through a private API preview. Open-weight Muse releases came afterwards, which I cover below, but the first model's weights weren't published.

The three modes, explained

ModeWhat it doesWhen to use it
InstantFast answers for everyday questionsQuick lookups, short drafts, casual chat
ThinkingReasons step by step before it repliesMaths, planning, harder questions
ContemplatingRuns several reasoning agents in parallel and combines their workComplex research or analysis where time matters less than quality

Contemplating was announced as rolling out gradually, so what you see may depend on your account and country. Meta says it uses multi-agent orchestration, meaning multiple reasoning passes happen at once instead of one long chain. That's a design choice other labs have also pursued, and it's the part of Muse Spark Meta itself emphasizes most.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assuming "Muse" means open source. Only some Muse models ship weights, and the licenses differ. Check the one for the exact model you plan to use.

How good is it, honestly?

Two kinds of evidence exist, and they point in slightly different directions. Meta's own launch benchmarks highlight visual reasoning, charts and health questions, areas where it reported competitive or leading results. Independent aggregators in April placed it behind the top frontier models on overall intelligence scores. Both can be true: a model can be strong on specific tests and still trail on a broad index.

Benchmarks also go stale within weeks. The best test is your own workload. If you use ChatGPT or Gemini today, run your three most common tasks through Meta AI and compare. Since Meta AI is free, the experiment costs nothing, and the plan comparisons in the ChatGPT Free vs Plus guide and the Gemini Free vs Pro guide show what you'd be comparing against.

The trust questions worth asking

A free assistant from an advertising company raises a reasonable question about data. Coverage of Muse Spark's launch noted open questions around privacy and revenue, since Meta's AI is woven into social apps. I don't have evidence of anything improper, and I'd avoid guessing. The practical advice is the same as for any assistant: check the privacy settings in the app, don't paste in anything sensitive, and assume conversations may be reviewed or used under the terms you accepted.
